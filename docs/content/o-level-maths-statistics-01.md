## Q1 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Find the mean of $3, 7, 9, 12, 9$.

**Options**

- A. $9$
- B. $8$
- C. $40$
- D. $10$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Mean = sum of the values ÷ number of values.

$$
\text{mean} = \frac{3 + 7 + 9 + 12 + 9}{5} = \frac{40}{5} = 8
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A is the median and also the mode (9 appears twice). Option D divides by 4 instead of 5: count the values carefully.

## Q2 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Find the median of $8, 2, 9, 4, 11, 7$.

**Options**

- A. $7.5$
- B. $6.5$
- C. $7$
- D. $8$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

First arrange the values in order:

$$
2,\ 4,\ 7,\ 8,\ 9,\ 11
$$

There are 6 values (an even number), so the median is halfway between the 3rd and 4th values:

$$
\text{median} = \frac{7 + 8}{2} = 7.5
$$

**Answer: A**

Option B takes the middle two numbers **without sorting** first ($\tfrac{9 + 4}{2}$). Always put the data in order before finding the median.

## Q3 · difficulty 1

The table shows the shoe sizes of students in a class.

| Shoe size          | 37  | 38  | 39  | 40  | 41  |
| ------------------ | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

What is the modal shoe size?

**Options**

- A. $12$
- B. $41$
- C. $39$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

The mode is the value that occurs **most often**. The highest frequency is 12, for shoe size 39. **Answer: C**

Option A gives the highest **frequency**, not the shoe size it belongs to. Option B is the largest shoe size.

## Q5 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The marks scored by 20 students in a short quiz are shown in the table.

| Mark      | 1   | 2   | 3   | 4   | 5   |
| --------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | 2   | 5   | 8   | 3   | 2   |

Find the mean mark.

**Options**

- A. $2.9$
- B. $3$
- C. $11.6$
- D. $4$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Multiply each mark by its frequency, add, then divide by the total frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum fx &= 1(2) + 2(5) + 3(8) + 4(3) + 5(2) = 2 + 10 + 24 + 12 + 10 = 58 \\
\text{mean} &= \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f} = \frac{58}{20} = 2.9
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer: A**

Option B is the mode (and the median). Option C divides by 5, the number of different marks, instead of 20, the number of students.

## Q6 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The mean of five numbers is $12$. Four of the numbers are $10, 15, 8$ and $14$. Find the fifth number.

**Options**

- A. $12$
- B. $11.75$
- C. $47$
- D. $13$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

If the mean of 5 numbers is 12, their total is $5 \times 12 = 60$.

The four known numbers add up to $10 + 15 + 8 + 14 = 47$.

$$
\text{fifth number} = 60 - 47 = 13
$$

**Answer: D**

Option B is the mean of the four known numbers. Option C is their total.

## Q7 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

In a class, the 20 boys have a mean mark of $60$ and the 30 girls have a mean mark of $70$. Find the mean mark of the whole class.

**Options**

- A. $65$
- B. $66$
- C. $64$
- D. $3300$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Find each group's total, then divide by the total number of students.

$$
\text{mean} = \frac{20 \times 60 + 30 \times 70}{20 + 30} = \frac{1200 + 2100}{50} = \frac{3300}{50} = 66
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A simply averages 60 and 70, which is only correct when the two groups are the same size. Option C swaps the group sizes.

## Q8 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

In a survey of $72$ students, $18$ said football is their favourite sport. What angle represents football on a pie chart?

**Options**

- A. $18^\circ$
- B. $25^\circ$
- C. $90^\circ$
- D. $45^\circ$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

The whole circle, $360^\circ$, represents all 72 students.

$$
\text{angle} = \frac{18}{72} \times 360^\circ = \frac{1}{4} \times 360^\circ = 90^\circ
$$

**Answer: C**

Option B is the **percentage** (25%), not the angle. Option A uses the number of students as the angle.

## Q9 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

On a pie chart, the sector for students who walk to school has an angle of $120^\circ$ and represents $40$ students. How many students are represented by the whole pie chart?

**Options**

- A. $40$
- B. $360$
- C. $3$
- D. $120$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

$120^\circ$ is $\dfrac{120}{360} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ of the circle, so 40 students are one third of the total.

$$
\text{total} = 40 \times 3 = 120 \text{ students}
$$

(Or: each student is $120^\circ \div 40 = 3^\circ$, so the total is $360^\circ \div 3^\circ = 120$.) **Answer: D**

Option C is the number of degrees for each student, not the number of students.

## Q10 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The table shows the times, in minutes, that 20 students took to travel to school.

| Time (minutes) | $0 \le t < 10$ | $10 \le t < 20$ | $20 \le t < 30$ |
| -------------- | -------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| Frequency      | 4              | 10              | 6               |

Estimate the mean time.

**Options**

- A. $16$ minutes
- B. $15$ minutes
- C. $10.7$ minutes
- D. $6.7$ minutes

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Use the **midpoint** of each class: 5, 15 and 25.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum fx &= 4(5) + 10(15) + 6(25) = 20 + 150 + 150 = 320 \\
\text{mean} &\approx \frac{320}{20} = 16 \text{ minutes}
\end{aligned}
$$

It is an estimate because we do not know the exact times inside each class. **Answer: A**

Option B is just the middle midpoint. Option C divides by 30, the top of the last class, instead of the 20 students.

## Q11 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

A bag contains $5$ red, $3$ blue and $2$ green balls. One ball is taken at random. What is the probability that it is blue?

**Options**

- A. $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- B. $\dfrac{3}{10}$
- C. $\dfrac{3}{7}$
- D. $\dfrac{1}{10}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

There are $5 + 3 + 2 = 10$ balls, and 3 of them are blue.

$$
P(\text{blue}) = \frac{\text{number of blue balls}}{\text{total number of balls}} = \frac{3}{10}
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A treats the three colours as equally likely. Option C divides by the number of balls that are **not** blue.

## Q12 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The probability that it will rain tomorrow is $0.35$. What is the probability that it will **not** rain tomorrow?

**Options**

- A. $0.35$
- B. $1.35$
- C. $0.65$
- D. $0.5$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

An event either happens or does not, so the two probabilities add up to 1.

$$
P(\text{not rain}) = 1 - 0.35 = 0.65
$$

**Answer: C**

Option B is impossible: a probability can never be more than 1.

## Q13 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Two fair dice are thrown and the scores are added. What is the probability that the total is $7$?

**Options**

- A. $\dfrac{1}{11}$
- B. $\dfrac{7}{36}$
- C. $\dfrac{1}{12}$
- D. $\dfrac{1}{6}$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

There are $6 \times 6 = 36$ equally likely outcomes. The ones that total 7 are

$$
(1,6),\ (2,5),\ (3,4),\ (4,3),\ (5,2),\ (6,1)
$$

That is 6 outcomes, so $P(\text{total } 7) = \dfrac{6}{36} = \dfrac{1}{6}$. **Answer: D**

Option C counts $(1,6)$ and $(6,1)$ as the same outcome, but they are different (first die 1, second die 6, or the other way round). Option A treats the 11 possible totals (2 to 12) as equally likely, which they are not.

## Q14 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Two fair coins are tossed. What is the probability of getting **at least one** head?

**Options**

- A. $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- B. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- C. $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- D. $\dfrac{2}{3}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

The equally likely outcomes are HH, HT, TH and TT. Three of the four contain at least one head.

$$
P(\text{at least one head}) = \frac{3}{4}
$$

(Or: $1 - P(\text{no heads}) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{3}{4}$.) **Answer: A**

Option D lists only HH, HT and TT, forgetting that HT and TH are different outcomes.

## Q15 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

A bag contains $4$ red and $6$ blue counters. Two counters are taken out one after the other **without replacement**. What is the probability that both are red?

**Options**

- A. $\dfrac{4}{25}$
- B. $\dfrac{2}{15}$
- C. $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- D. $\dfrac{7}{19}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

For the first counter, 4 of the 10 are red. If it is red, only 3 red counters are left among 9.

$$
P(\text{both red}) = \frac{4}{10} \times \frac{3}{9} = \frac{12}{90} = \frac{2}{15}
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A is the answer **with** replacement ($\tfrac{4}{10} \times \tfrac{4}{10}$). Option C is only the probability for the first counter. Option D adds the fractions' tops and bottoms, which is never correct.

## Q16 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

$A$ and $B$ are independent events with $P(A) = 0.4$ and $P(B) = 0.5$. Find $P(A \text{ and } B)$.

**Options**

- A. $0.9$
- B. $0.1$
- C. $0.2$
- D. $0.45$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

For independent events, multiply the probabilities.

$$
P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B) = 0.4 \times 0.5 = 0.2
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A adds them, which is the rule for "or" with events that cannot happen together, not for "and".

## Q17 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

A fair die is thrown $120$ times. How many times would you expect to get a $6$?

**Options**

- A. $6$
- B. $12$
- C. $720$
- D. $20$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

Expected frequency = probability × number of trials.

$$
\frac{1}{6} \times 120 = 20
$$

**Answer: D**

Option C multiplies by 6 instead of dividing.

## Q18 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

For a set of marks, the lower quartile is $34$ and the upper quartile is $58$. Find the interquartile range.

**Options**

- A. $46$
- B. $24$
- C. $92$
- D. $12$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Interquartile range = upper quartile − lower quartile.

$$
58 - 34 = 24
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A is halfway between the quartiles. Option D is half the interquartile range (the semi-interquartile range).

## Q19 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

In a histogram, the class $10 \le x < 30$ has a frequency of $40$. What is its frequency density?

**Options**

- A. $40$
- B. $800$
- C. $2$
- D. $0.5$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width. The class width is $30 - 10 = 20$.

$$
\frac{40}{20} = 2
$$

In a histogram the **area** of each bar (width × height) shows the frequency. **Answer: C**

Option B multiplies instead of dividing. Option D divides the class width by the frequency.

## Q20 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The mean of a set of numbers is $12$. Every number in the set is increased by $5$. What is the new mean?

**Options**

- A. $17$
- B. $12$
- C. $60$
- D. $2.4$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Adding the same amount to every value moves the whole set up by that amount, so the mean also goes up by 5.

$$
12 + 5 = 17
$$

(The range does not change, because the largest and smallest values both go up by 5.) **Answer: A**

Option C multiplies the mean by 5, which is what would happen if every number were **multiplied** by 5.
