## Q1 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

A wave has a frequency of $50\,\text{Hz}$ and a wavelength of $0.4\,\text{m}$. What is the speed of the wave?

**Options**

- A. $125\,\text{m s}^{-1}$
- B. $20\,\text{m s}^{-1}$
- C. $0.008\,\text{m s}^{-1}$
- D. $50.4\,\text{m s}^{-1}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Wave speed = frequency × wavelength.

$$
v = f\lambda = 50\,\text{Hz} \times 0.4\,\text{m} = 20\,\text{m s}^{-1}
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A divides the frequency by the wavelength, and option C divides the wavelength by the frequency. Option D adds the numbers.

## Q2 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Which of these is a longitudinal wave?

**Options**

- A. Light
- B. Radio waves
- C. Ripples on water
- D. Sound in air

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

In a longitudinal wave the particles vibrate **along** the direction the wave travels, making compressions and rarefactions. Sound in air travels this way.

Light and radio waves are electromagnetic waves, which are transverse. Ripples on water are also treated as transverse at O Level: the water moves up and down while the wave moves across.

**Answer: D**

## Q3 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The distance between two neighbouring crests of a wave is called the

**Options**

- A. wavelength
- B. amplitude
- C. frequency
- D. period

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

The wavelength $\lambda$ is the distance between two neighbouring points vibrating in step, such as crest to crest or trough to trough.

The amplitude is the greatest displacement from the rest position. The frequency is the number of waves per second, and the period is the time for one wave. Neither of these last two is a distance.

**Answer: A**

## Q4 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A wave has a period of $0.02\,\text{s}$. What is its frequency?

**Options**

- A. $0.02\,\text{Hz}$
- B. $20\,\text{Hz}$
- C. $50\,\text{Hz}$
- D. $200\,\text{Hz}$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Frequency is the number of waves each second, so it is 1 divided by the period.

$$
f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{0.02\,\text{s}} = 50\,\text{Hz}
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A gives the period again with the wrong unit.

## Q5 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A radio station broadcasts at a frequency of $100\,\text{MHz}$. The speed of radio waves is $3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m s}^{-1}$. What is the wavelength of the waves?

**Options**

- A. $0.33\,\text{m}$
- B. $30\,\text{m}$
- C. $3.0\,\text{m}$
- D. $3.0 \times 10^{16}\,\text{m}$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Change the frequency to hertz first: $100\,\text{MHz} = 100 \times 10^6\,\text{Hz} = 1.0 \times 10^8\,\text{Hz}$.

$$
\lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m s}^{-1}}{1.0 \times 10^8\,\text{Hz}} = 3.0\,\text{m}
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A turns the fraction upside down. Option D multiplies instead of dividing.

## Q6 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A student stands in front of a cliff and claps. The echo is heard $2.0\,\text{s}$ later. The speed of sound in air is $340\,\text{m s}^{-1}$. How far is the student from the cliff?

**Options**

- A. $340\,\text{m}$
- B. $680\,\text{m}$
- C. $170\,\text{m}$
- D. $1360\,\text{m}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

In $2.0\,\text{s}$ the sound travels to the cliff **and back**.

$$
\text{distance travelled} = vt = 340 \times 2.0 = 680\,\text{m}
$$

The cliff is half of this distance away:

$$
d = \frac{680}{2} = 340\,\text{m}
$$

**Answer: A**

Option B forgets that the sound goes there and back.

## Q7 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Which part of the electromagnetic spectrum has the longest wavelength?

**Options**

- A. Gamma rays
- B. X-rays
- C. Ultraviolet
- D. Radio waves

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

In order of increasing wavelength, the spectrum is: gamma rays, X-rays, ultraviolet, visible light, infrared, microwaves, radio waves.

Radio waves have the longest wavelength and the lowest frequency. Gamma rays have the shortest wavelength.

**Answer: D**

## Q8 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A ray of light passes from air into glass. Which statement is correct?

**Options**

- A. Its speed increases and its frequency stays the same.
- B. Its speed decreases and its wavelength decreases.
- C. Its frequency decreases and its speed stays the same.
- D. Its wavelength increases and its speed decreases.

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Light travels more slowly in glass than in air. The **frequency never changes** when a wave enters a new medium, because it is set by the source.

Since $v = f\lambda$ and $f$ is fixed, a smaller speed means a smaller wavelength.

**Answer: B**

Option D cannot be right: with the same frequency, a slower wave must have a shorter wavelength.

## Q9 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A ray of light in air strikes a glass block at an angle of incidence of $60^\circ$. The angle of refraction in the glass is $35^\circ$. What is the refractive index of the glass? (Use $\sin 60^\circ = 0.866$ and $\sin 35^\circ = 0.574$.)

**Options**

- A. $1.51$
- B. $1.71$
- C. $0.66$
- D. $0.57$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Use Snell's law:

$$
n = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \frac{\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 35^\circ} = \frac{0.866}{0.574} = 1.51
$$

**Answer: A**

Option B divides the angles themselves ($60 \div 35$). Option C turns the fraction upside down. A refractive index for glass is always greater than 1.

## Q10 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

The refractive index of a glass is $1.5$. What is the critical angle for light passing from this glass into air?

**Options**

- A. $30^\circ$
- B. $48^\circ$
- C. $42^\circ$
- D. $56^\circ$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

At the critical angle $c$, the angle of refraction in air is $90^\circ$. This gives

$$
\sin c = \frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{1.5} = 0.667
$$

$$
c = \sin^{-1}(0.667) \approx 42^\circ
$$

**Answer: C**

Option B is $90^\circ - 42^\circ$. Option D comes from $\tan^{-1}(1.5)$, which is the wrong rule.

## Q11 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Total internal reflection of light happens when light

**Options**

- A. goes from a less dense medium into a denser medium at a large angle of incidence.
- B. goes from a denser medium towards a less dense medium at an angle of incidence smaller than the critical angle.
- C. strikes a plane mirror along the normal.
- D. goes from a denser medium towards a less dense medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle.

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

Two conditions are both needed:

1. the light is in the **denser** medium (for example glass or water) heading towards a less dense one (for example air);
2. the angle of incidence is **greater than the critical angle**.

Then no light is refracted out, and all of it is reflected back inside.

**Answer: D**

In option B the angle is too small, so most of the light refracts out. Option C describes ordinary reflection from a mirror.

## Q12 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The pitch of a musical note depends on its

**Options**

- A. amplitude
- B. frequency
- C. speed
- D. distance from the listener

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

A higher frequency gives a higher pitch. A larger amplitude gives a **louder** note, not a higher one.

All notes travel through air at the same speed, so speed does not decide pitch.

**Answer: B**

## Q13 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Sound cannot travel through

**Options**

- A. water
- B. steel
- C. a vacuum
- D. air

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Sound is a mechanical wave: it is passed on by particles of a material vibrating and pushing their neighbours. A vacuum has no particles, so sound cannot travel through it.

Sound travels through liquids and solids, and faster in them than in air.

**Answer: C**

This is why astronauts in space use radio, which is an electromagnetic wave and can cross a vacuum.

## Q14 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Ultrasound is sound with a frequency

**Options**

- A. above $20\,000\,\text{Hz}$
- B. below $20\,\text{Hz}$
- C. between $20\,\text{Hz}$ and $20\,000\,\text{Hz}$
- D. of exactly $340\,\text{Hz}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Humans hear frequencies from about $20\,\text{Hz}$ to $20\,000\,\text{Hz}$ ($20\,\text{kHz}$). Ultrasound is sound **above** the range of human hearing, so above $20\,\text{kHz}$.

**Answer: A**

Option B describes infrasound, and option C is the range we can hear. Option D confuses the speed of sound in air, $340\,\text{m s}^{-1}$, with a frequency.

## Q15 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Water waves pass through a gap in a barrier. The waves spread out (diffract) the most when the width of the gap is

**Options**

- A. much larger than the wavelength.
- B. about the same as the wavelength.
- C. any size, because diffraction does not depend on the gap.
- D. smallest compared with the amplitude of the waves.

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Diffraction is greatest when the gap is about the same size as the wavelength. Through a very wide gap the waves pass almost straight on, with only a little spreading at the edges.

**Answer: B**

This is why we can hear sound around a doorway (wavelength about $1\,\text{m}$) but cannot see around it (light's wavelength is far smaller than the doorway).

## Q16 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

In a ripple tank, 10 waves pass a point in $2.0\,\text{s}$. The distance between neighbouring crests is $3.0\,\text{cm}$. What is the speed of the waves?

**Options**

- A. $60\,\text{cm s}^{-1}$
- B. $6.0\,\text{cm s}^{-1}$
- C. $0.6\,\text{cm s}^{-1}$
- D. $15\,\text{cm s}^{-1}$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

First find the frequency, which is the number of waves per second:

$$
f = \frac{10}{2.0\,\text{s}} = 5.0\,\text{Hz}
$$

The wavelength is $3.0\,\text{cm}$, so

$$
v = f\lambda = 5.0 \times 3.0 = 15\,\text{cm s}^{-1}
$$

**Answer: D**

Option A multiplies all three numbers. Option C divides the wavelength by the frequency.

## Q17 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Which electromagnetic radiation is used to kill bacteria on surgical instruments and to treat cancer?

**Options**

- A. Gamma rays
- B. Infrared
- C. Microwaves
- D. Radio waves

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Gamma rays carry the most energy of all electromagnetic waves. They can pass through packaging and kill living cells, so they are used to sterilise instruments and to destroy cancer cells.

Infrared is used for heating and remote controls, microwaves for cooking and mobile phones, and radio waves for broadcasting.

**Answer: A**

## Q18 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

A wave on a rope is drawn on a graph of displacement against distance. Each crest is $5\,\text{cm}$ above the rest line and each trough is $5\,\text{cm}$ below it. Exactly four complete waves fit along $2.0\,\text{m}$ of the rope. What are the amplitude and the wavelength?

**Options**

- A. amplitude $10\,\text{cm}$, wavelength $0.5\,\text{m}$
- B. amplitude $5\,\text{cm}$, wavelength $2.0\,\text{m}$
- C. amplitude $10\,\text{cm}$, wavelength $8.0\,\text{m}$
- D. amplitude $5\,\text{cm}$, wavelength $0.5\,\text{m}$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

The amplitude is measured from the **rest line** to a crest, not from crest to trough, so it is $5\,\text{cm}$.

Four whole waves fill $2.0\,\text{m}$, so one wavelength is

$$
\lambda = \frac{2.0\,\text{m}}{4} = 0.5\,\text{m}
$$

**Answer: D**

Options A and C use the crest-to-trough height of $10\,\text{cm}$. Option B takes the whole length as one wavelength, and option C multiplies by 4 instead of dividing.

## Q19 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

A ship sends a pulse of sound straight down to the sea bed. The echo returns $0.80\,\text{s}$ later. The speed of sound in sea water is $1500\,\text{m s}^{-1}$. How deep is the sea?

**Options**

- A. $1200\,\text{m}$
- B. $1875\,\text{m}$
- C. $600\,\text{m}$
- D. $300\,\text{m}$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

The pulse goes down to the sea bed and back up again.

$$
\text{distance travelled} = vt = 1500 \times 0.80 = 1200\,\text{m}
$$

$$
\text{depth} = \frac{1200}{2} = 600\,\text{m}
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A forgets to halve the distance. Option B divides the speed by the time, and option D halves the distance twice.

## Q20 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

All electromagnetic waves travelling in a vacuum have the same

**Options**

- A. frequency
- B. speed
- C. wavelength
- D. energy

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Every electromagnetic wave, from radio waves to gamma rays, travels through a vacuum at the speed of light, $3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m s}^{-1}$.

They differ in frequency and wavelength, and so in the energy they carry: gamma rays have the highest frequency and the most energy.

**Answer: B**
