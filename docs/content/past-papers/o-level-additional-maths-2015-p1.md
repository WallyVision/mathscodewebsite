## Q1 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

$4^{\frac{3}{2}} =$

**Options**

- A. $6$
- B. $8$
- C. $12$
- D. $16$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Take the square root first, then cube: $4^{3/2} = \left(\sqrt{4}\right)^3 = 2^3 = 8$.

**Answer: B**

Option A multiplies $4 \times \frac{3}{2}$, which is not how indices work.

## Q12 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The sum to infinity of a geometric progression is 3 and the first term is 2. The value of the common ratio is

**Options**

- A. $3$
- B. $-\frac{1}{3}$
- C. $\frac{1}{3}$
- D. $3$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

$$
\frac{a}{1 - r} = 3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{2}{1 - r} = 3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 1 - r = \frac{2}{3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad r = \frac{1}{3}
$$

**Answer: C**

A ratio of 3 is impossible: a sum to infinity exists only when $-1 < r < 1$. (Options A and D are printed the same in the source.)

## Q13 · difficulty 2

The first three terms, in ascending powers of $x$, in the binomial expansion of $(1 - 2x)^{-1}$ are

## Q25 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The value of $p$ for which the points $(2, 1)$, $(6, 3)$ and $(4, p)$ are collinear is

**Options**

- A. $2$
- B. $1$
- C. $-1$
- D. $-2$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

The gradient from $(2, 1)$ to $(6, 3)$ is $\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$. The gradient from $(2, 1)$ to $(4, p)$ must be the same:

$$
\frac{p - 1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad p = 2
$$

(Also: $x = 4$ is midway between 2 and 6, so $p$ is midway between 1 and 3.)

**Answer: A**

## Q32 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

A region is bounded by the inequalities $y \ge 0$, $y \le 2x$ and $2x + y \le 8$. The maximum value of $2x - y$ constrained by the inequalities is

**Options**

- A. $3$
- B. $7$
- C. $8$
- D. $15$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

The region is a triangle with corners:

- $(0, 0)$: $2x - y = 0$;
- $y = 0$ and $2x + y = 8$: $(4, 0)$, value $8$;
- $y = 2x$ and $2x + y = 8$: $(2, 4)$, value $4 - 4 = 0$.

The maximum is 8, at $(4, 0)$.

**Answer: C**

(The source refers to a shaded diagram, but the corners come from the inequalities alone.)

## Q37 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

An invariant line under the transformation $M$, where $M = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, is

**Options**

- A. $2x - y = 0$
- B. $2x + y = 0$
- C. $3x + y = 0$
- D. $2x + 2y = 0$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Try lines through the origin, $y = mx$. The point $(x, mx)$ maps to

$$
\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ mx \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} (3 + m)x \\ (2 + 2m)x \end{pmatrix}
$$

For the image to lie on the same line: $2 + 2m = m(3 + m)$, so $m^2 + m - 2 = 0$, giving $m = 1$ or $m = -2$.

$y = -2x$ is the line $2x + y = 0$.

Check: $(1, -2) \to (3 - 2, 2 - 4) = (1, -2)$, still on the line. ✓

**Answer: B**
