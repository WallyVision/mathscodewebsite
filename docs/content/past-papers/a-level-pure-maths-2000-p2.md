## 1 · 10 marks

**Question**

(i) Find an equation of the tangent to the curve $y = \ln(4 + x^2)$ at the point where $x = 1$.

(ii) Solve the differential equation $\dfrac{dy}{dx} - xy - x = 0$, given that $y = 0$ when $x = 0$.

**Solution**

**(i)** $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2x}{4 + x^2}$, which is $\frac{2}{5}$ at $x = 1$. The point is $(1, \ln 5)$:

$$
y - \ln 5 = \frac{2}{5}(x - 1) \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad y = \frac{2}{5}x - \frac{2}{5} + \ln 5
$$

**(ii)** $\frac{dy}{dx} = x(y + 1)$. Separate the variables:

$$
\int\frac{dy}{y + 1} = \int x\,dx \quad\Rightarrow\quad \ln|y + 1| = \frac{x^2}{2} + c \quad\Rightarrow\quad y + 1 = Ae^{x^2/2}
$$

$y = 0$ when $x = 0$ gives $A = 1$, so $y = e^{x^2/2} - 1$.

## 2 · 8 marks

**Question**

Given that $f$ is a periodic function of period 5 and that

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} -x^2 - x + 6, & 0 \le x \le 2 \\ ax - 4, & 2 \le x \le 5 \end{cases}
$$

(a) find the value of $a$ for which $f$ is continuous,

(b) find the value of $f(-1)$,

(c) sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$ in the interval $-3 \le x \le 7$.

**Solution**

**(a)** At $x = 2$ both pieces must agree: $-4 - 2 + 6 = 0 = 2a - 4$, so $a = 2$.

(This also joins up across the period: $f(5) = 10 - 4 = 6 = f(0)$. ✓)

**(b)** $f(-1) = f(-1 + 5) = f(4) = 2(4) - 4 = 4$.

**(c)** One period, from $0$ to $5$:

- $0 \le x \le 2$: the parabola $y = -x^2 - x + 6 = -(x + 3)(x - 2)$, falling from $(0, 6)$ to $(2, 0)$;
- $2 \le x \le 5$: the straight line $y = 2x - 4$, rising from $(2, 0)$ to $(5, 6)$.

Repeat it with period 5. On $-3 \le x \le 0$ the graph is the line from $(-3, 0)$ up to $(0, 6)$, and on $5 \le x \le 7$ it is the parabolic arc from $(5, 6)$ down to $(7, 0)$. The graph is continuous, with peaks at $x = 0$ and $x = 5$ and zeros at $x = -3, 2, 7$.

## 3 · 9 marks

**Question**

(i) Find $n$, given that $\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5 - 2r) = -165$.

(ii) The sum of the first $n$ terms of a series is $9\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{3^n}\right)$.

(a) Find the $n$th term of the series.

(b) Show that the series is a geometric series and find its sum to infinity.

**Solution**

**(i)**

$$
\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5 - 2r) = 5n - 2 \cdot \frac{n(n + 1)}{2} = 4n - n^2 = -165
$$

$$
n^2 - 4n - 165 = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (n - 15)(n + 11) = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad n = 15
$$

**(ii)(a)**

$$
u_n = S_n - S_{n - 1} = 9\left(\frac{1}{3^{n - 1}} - \frac{1}{3^n}\right) = 9 \cdot \frac{3 - 1}{3^n} = \frac{18}{3^n}
$$

(This also gives $u_1 = 6 = S_1$ ✓.)

**(ii)(b)** $\frac{u_{n + 1}}{u_n} = \frac{1}{3}$ for every $n$, so the series is geometric with $a = 6$ and $r = \frac{1}{3}$.

$$
S_\infty = \frac{6}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = 9 \qquad (\text{also } 9(1 - 3^{-n}) \to 9)
$$

## 4 · 10 marks

**Question**

(i) Sketch the curve $y = 3x^2 - 12x$ and hence find the area of the region bounded by the curve and the lines $y = 0$, $x = 0$ and $x = 5$.

(ii) Find the volume generated by rotating completely, about the $x$-axis, the area bounded by the curve $x = 3(y^2 - 1)$ and the lines $x = 0$ and $x = 6$. Find also the $x$-coordinate of the centroid of the solid generated.

**Solution**

**(i)** $y = 3x(x - 4)$ is a U-shaped parabola crossing the $x$-axis at $0$ and $4$, with minimum $(2, -12)$. It is below the axis on $[0, 4]$ and above it on $[4, 5]$, so split the integral. With $\int(3x^2 - 12x)\,dx = x^3 - 6x^2$:

$$
\int_0^4 = (64 - 96) - 0 = -32, \qquad \int_4^5 = (125 - 150) - (-32) = 7
$$

$$
\text{Area} = 32 + 7 = 39 \text{ square units}
$$

**(ii)** $y^2 = \frac{x}{3} + 1$:

$$
V = \pi\int_0^6 \left(\frac{x}{3} + 1\right)dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{6} + x\right]_0^6 = 12\pi
$$

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\pi\int_0^6 x\left(\frac{x}{3} + 1\right)dx}{V} = \frac{\left[\frac{x^3}{9} + \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^6}{12} = \frac{24 + 18}{12} = 3.5
$$

## 5 · 11 marks

**Question**

The equations of two circles $C_1$ and $C_2$ are $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ and $x^2 + y^2 - 2x = 0$ respectively.

(a) Show that the circles $C_1$ and $C_2$ touch each other internally, and find an equation of the common tangent at the point of contact.

(b) Find the equations of the tangents to the circle $C_1$ which are perpendicular to the common tangent.

(c) Find the area between the two circles.

**Solution**

**(a)** $C_1$ has centre $(0, 0)$ and radius 2. $C_2$ is $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1$, with centre $(1, 0)$ and radius 1.

The distance between the centres is $1 = 2 - 1$, the difference of the radii, so the circles touch internally. They touch at $(2, 0)$, on the line of centres. The common tangent there is perpendicular to the $x$-axis: $x = 2$.

**(b)** Tangents perpendicular to $x = 2$ are horizontal, and those touching $C_1$ are $y = 2$ and $y = -2$.

**(c)** $\pi(2^2) - \pi(1^2) = 3\pi$ square units.

## 6

**Question**

(a) Write the complex number $\dfrac{1 + \sqrt{3}\,i}{2 - 2i}$ in the form $r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$, where $\theta$ is in radians.

(b) $P_1$ and $P_2$ are the points representing the complex numbers $3 + i$ and $-1 + 3i$ respectively. Show that $OP_1$ is perpendicular to $OP_2$, where $O$ is the origin.

**Solution**

**(a)** $1 + \sqrt{3}\,i$ has modulus 2 and argument $\frac{\pi}{3}$. $2 - 2i$ has modulus $2\sqrt{2}$ and argument $-\frac{\pi}{4}$. Divide the moduli and subtract the arguments:

$$
\frac{1 + \sqrt{3}\,i}{2 - 2i} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\cos\frac{7\pi}{12} + i\sin\frac{7\pi}{12}\right)
$$

**(b)** $\overrightarrow{OP_1} \cdot \overrightarrow{OP_2} = (3)(-1) + (1)(3) = 0$, so they are perpendicular.

(Or: $\frac{-1 + 3i}{3 + i} = \frac{(-1 + 3i)(3 - i)}{10} = \frac{10i}{10} = i$, and multiplying by $i$ is a rotation through $90^\circ$.)

**Note:** the source does not give the marks for this question.

## 7 · 13 marks

**Question**

Given that the position vectors of the points $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ relative to the origin $O$ are $\mathbf{a} = 3\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{b} = 2\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{c} = 4\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k}$ and $\mathbf{d} = 3\mathbf{i} + 7\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k}$ respectively, find

(a) a vector equation of the line $AB$,

(b) Cartesian equations of the line $l_1$ through $A$ and $D$,

(c) a Cartesian equation of the plane $\pi$ containing $A$, $B$ and $C$,

(d) the angle between the plane $\pi$ and the line $l_1$, to two decimal places.

**Solution**

**(a)** $\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 4, -1)$: $\mathbf{r} = 3\mathbf{k} + \lambda(2\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j} - \mathbf{k})$.

**(b)** $\overrightarrow{AD} = (3, 7, 2)$:

$$
\frac{x}{3} = \frac{y}{7} = \frac{z - 3}{2}
$$

**(c)** $\overrightarrow{AC} = (4, 3, -2)$:

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = (-8 + 3,\ -4 + 4,\ 6 - 16) = (-5, 0, -10) \parallel (1, 0, 2)
$$

Through $A(0, 0, 3)$: $x + 2z = 6$. (Check: $B$ gives $2 + 4 = 6$ ✓ and $C$ gives $4 + 2 = 6$ ✓.)

**(d)** With $\mathbf{n} = (1, 0, 2)$ and $\mathbf{d} = (3, 7, 2)$:

$$
\sin\phi = \frac{|\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{d}|}{|\mathbf{n}||\mathbf{d}|} = \frac{|3 + 4|}{\sqrt{5}\sqrt{62}} = \frac{7}{\sqrt{310}} \approx 0.3976 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \phi \approx 23.43^\circ
$$

## 8 · 11 marks

**Question**

(i) Show that $\cos(2\tan^{-1}x) = \dfrac{1 - x^2}{1 + x^2}$.

(ii) Find, in radians, the general solution of the equation $\sin 5x + \sin 3x = 0$.

(iii) Express $7\cos\theta - 5\sin\theta$ in the form $R\cos(\theta + \lambda)$, where $R > 0$ and $0^\circ < \lambda < 90^\circ$, giving $R$ and $\lambda$ correct to one decimal place. Hence find the value of $\theta$ in the interval $0^\circ < \theta < 360^\circ$ for which $7\cos\theta - 5\sin\theta = 8.6$.

**Solution**

**(i)** Let $t = \tan^{-1}x$, so $\tan t = x$. Then

$$
\cos 2t = \frac{1 - \tan^2 t}{1 + \tan^2 t} = \frac{1 - x^2}{1 + x^2} \ ✓
$$

**(ii)** $\sin 5x + \sin 3x = 2\sin 4x\cos x = 0$:

- $\sin 4x = 0$: $x = \frac{n\pi}{4}$;
- $\cos x = 0$: $x = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$, which is already included above.

$$
x = \frac{n\pi}{4}, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

**(iii)** $R\cos\lambda = 7$ and $R\sin\lambda = 5$:

$$
R = \sqrt{74} \approx 8.6, \qquad \lambda = \tan^{-1}\frac{5}{7} \approx 35.5^\circ
$$

With $R = 8.6$, the equation becomes $\cos(\theta + 35.5^\circ) = 1$, so $\theta + 35.5^\circ = 360^\circ$ and

$$
\theta \approx 324.5^\circ
$$

(With the unrounded $R = 8.602$, $\cos(\theta + 35.54^\circ) = 0.9997$ gives two very close solutions, $\theta \approx 323.1^\circ$ and $325.8^\circ$. The question expects the single value from $R = 8.6$.)

## 9 · 8 marks

**Question**

Find

(a) $\displaystyle\int xe^{x^2 + 1}\,dx$,

(b) $\displaystyle\int_0^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{x}{1 - x}}\,dx$, using the substitution $x = \cos^2 t$.

**Solution**

**(a)** $\frac{d}{dx}e^{x^2 + 1} = 2xe^{x^2 + 1}$, so

$$
\int xe^{x^2 + 1}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}e^{x^2 + 1} + c
$$

**(b)** $dx = -2\cos t\sin t\,dt$ and $\sqrt{\frac{\cos^2 t}{\sin^2 t}} = \frac{\cos t}{\sin t}$. The limits $x = 0, \frac{1}{2}$ become $t = \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{4}$:

$$
\int_{\pi/2}^{\pi/4}\frac{\cos t}{\sin t}(-2\cos t\sin t)\,dt = \int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2} 2\cos^2 t\,dt = \int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2}(1 + \cos 2t)\,dt
$$

$$
= \left[t + \frac{1}{2}\sin 2t\right]_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2} = \frac{\pi}{2} - \left(\frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{2} \approx 0.285
$$

## 10 · 10 marks

**Question**

(i) A person sends messages by using six flags, of which one is blue, two are white and three are red. The messages are sent by hoisting the flags in a row, the message being conveyed by the order in which the colours of the flags are arranged. How many different messages can be sent by using

(a) all the six flags,

(b) five flags only?

(ii) Given that $x = e^{3t}$ and $y = e^{2t + 3}$, find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ in terms of $x$.

**Solution**

**(i)(a)** $\dfrac{6!}{1!\,2!\,3!} = \dfrac{720}{12} = 60$.

**(i)(b)** Leave out one flag:

| Left out | Flags used | Arrangements             |
| -------- | ---------- | ------------------------ |
| blue     | W W R R R  | $\frac{5!}{2!\,3!} = 10$ |
| a white  | B W R R R  | $\frac{5!}{3!} = 20$     |
| a red    | B W W R R  | $\frac{5!}{2!\,2!} = 30$ |

Total: $60$ messages.

**(ii)**

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2e^{2t + 3}}{3e^{3t}} = \frac{2}{3}e^{3 - t}
$$

Since $x = e^{3t}$, $e^{-t} = x^{-1/3}$, so

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2e^3}{3}x^{-1/3} = \frac{2e^3}{3\sqrt[3]{x}}
$$
