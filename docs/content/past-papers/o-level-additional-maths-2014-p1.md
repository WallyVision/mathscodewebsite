## Q1 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The value of $x$ for which $8^x = \frac{1}{2}$ is

**Options**

- A. $16$
- B. $\frac{1}{4}$
- C. $-\frac{1}{3}$
- D. $-4$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Write both sides as powers of 2: $8 = 2^3$ and $\frac{1}{2} = 2^{-1}$.

$$
2^{3x} = 2^{-1} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x = -1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = -\frac{1}{3}
$$

**Answer: C**

$\frac{\log 8}{\log 16} =$

**Options**

- A. $-\frac{1}{2}$
- B. $\frac{1}{2}$
- C. $\frac{3}{4}$
- D. $2$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

$$
\frac{\log 2^3}{\log 2^4} = \frac{3\log 2}{4\log 2} = \frac{3}{4}
$$

**Answer: C**

A common mistake is to treat this as $\log 8 - \log 16$ or $\log \frac{8}{16}$. Neither is the same as dividing two logs.

## Q3 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

$\frac{1}{3 - \sqrt{2}}$ expressed in the form $a + b\sqrt{c}$ is

**Options**

- A. $\frac{3}{7} - \frac{1}{7}\sqrt{2}$
- B. $\frac{3}{7} + \frac{1}{7}\sqrt{2}$
- C. $\frac{3}{5} - \frac{1}{5}\sqrt{2}$
- D. $\frac{3}{5} + \frac{1}{5}\sqrt{2}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate $3 + \sqrt{2}$:

$$
\frac{3 + \sqrt{2}}{(3 - \sqrt{2})(3 + \sqrt{2})} = \frac{3 + \sqrt{2}}{9 - 2} = \frac{3}{7} + \frac{1}{7}\sqrt{2}
$$

**Answer: B**

C and D use $9 - 4$: the square of $\sqrt{2}$ is 2, not 4.

## Q8 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The equation $2x^2 + x - 2 = 0$ has roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$. The value of $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta}$ is

**Options**

- A. $1$
- B. $\frac{1}{2}$
- C. $-\frac{1}{2}$
- D. $-1$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

$\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{1}{2}$ and $\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} = -1$.

$$
\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} = \frac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta} = \frac{-\frac{1}{2}}{-1} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

**Answer: B**

## Q10 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The solution set of values of $x$ for which $2x^2 + 5x - 3 < 0$ is

**Options**

- A. $\left\{x : -\frac{1}{2} \le x \le 3\right\}$
- B. $\left\{x : -3 \le x \le \frac{1}{2}\right\}$
- C. $\left\{x : x \le -3 \ \cup\ x \ge \frac{1}{2}\right\}$
- D. $\left\{x : x \le -\frac{1}{2} \ \cup\ x \ge 3\right\}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Factorise: $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = (2x - 1)(x + 3)$. The roots are $x = \frac{1}{2}$ and $x = -3$. The parabola opens upwards, so it is negative **between** the roots:

$$
-3 < x < \frac{1}{2}
$$

**Answer: B**

C is where the expression is positive. A and D come from wrong signs in the factors. (The options are printed with $\le$; strictly the end points are not included.)

## Q12 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The function $f$ is defined as $f : x \mapsto \frac{1 + x}{1 - 2x}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $x \ne \frac{1}{2}$. Then the inverse function is $f^{-1} : x \mapsto$

**Options**

- A. $\frac{x - 1}{1 + 2x}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}, x \ne -\frac{1}{2}$
- B. $\frac{1 - x}{1 - 2x}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}, x \ne -\frac{1}{2}$
- C. $\frac{1 - x}{1 - 2x}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}, x \ne \frac{1}{2}$
- D. $\frac{x - 1}{1 - 2x}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}, x \ne \frac{1}{2}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Let $y = \frac{1 + x}{1 - 2x}$ and make $x$ the subject:

$$
y - 2xy = 1 + x \quad\Rightarrow\quad y - 1 = x + 2xy = x(1 + 2y) \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{y - 1}{1 + 2y}
$$

So $f^{-1}(x) = \frac{x - 1}{1 + 2x}$, undefined at $x = -\frac{1}{2}$.

Check: $f(1) = \frac{2}{-1} = -2$ and $f^{-1}(-2) = \frac{-3}{-3} = 1$. ✓

**Answer: A**

**Options**

- A. $-2$

The middle term of an A.P. is the average of its neighbours:

$$

## Q25 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The operation $*$ is defined on $\{p, q, r, s\}$ by the table:

| $*$   | p   | q   | r   | s   |
| ----- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **p** | r   | p   | s   | q   |
| **q** | p   | q   | r   | s   |
| **r** | s   | r   | q   | p   |
| **s** | q   | s   | p   | r   |

From the operation table, the inverse of $q$ is

**Options**

- A. p
- B. q
- C. r
- D. s

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

First find the identity. The row for $q$ repeats the heading row ($q * p = p$, $q * q = q$, …), and so does the column for $q$. So $q$ is the identity element.

The inverse of $x$ is the element that combines with $x$ to give the identity. Since $q * q = q$, the inverse of $q$ is $q$ itself. (The identity is always its own inverse.)

**Answer: B**

## Q28 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Given that the matrix for the transformation $T$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, then the invariant point under $T$ is

**Options**

- A. $(0, 0)$
- B. $(2, 2)$
- C. $(0, -2)$
- D. $(2, -2)$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

An invariant point maps to itself:

$$
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x - 3y = x,\quad x + y = y
$$

So $y = 0$ and $x = 0$: only the origin is invariant.

**Answer: A**

Check B: $(2, 2) \to (2 - 6, 2 + 2) = (-4, 4)$, which is not $(2, 2)$.

## Q50 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Given that the rate of change of the radius of a sphere is $2\text{ cm s}^{-1}$, then the rate of change of the volume of the sphere, in $\text{cm}^3\text{ s}^{-1}$, at the instant when the radius is 3 cm is

[Volume of a sphere is $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$]

**Options**

- A. $72\pi$
- B. $48\pi$
- C. $36\pi$
- D. $\frac{32}{3}\pi$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

$\frac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^2$, so by the chain rule

$$
