## Q1 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

When $\frac{1}{2}$ of $6 - \left(\frac{3}{2} \times 4\right)$ is evaluated, the result is

**Options**

- A. $3$
- B. $6$
- C. $0$
- D. $-3$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

By BODMAS, work out the bracket and the "of" before subtracting:

$$
\frac{1}{2} \times 6 - \left(\frac{3}{2} \times 4\right) = 3 - 6 = -3
$$

**Answer: D**

## Q6 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Expressing $204\,865$ to three significant figures gives

**Options**

- A. $205\,000$
- B. $204\,800$
- C. $204\,900$
- D. $200\,900$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

The first three significant figures are $2, 0, 4$: $204|865$. The next digit is 8, so round the 4 up to 5 and fill with zeros:

$$
204\,865 \approx 205\,000 \text{ (3 s.f.)}
$$

**Answer: A**

Option C is correct to 4 significant figures.

## Q11 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The number of subsets of the set $T = \{a, b, c\}$ is

**Options**

- A. $3$
- B. $6$
- C. $8$
- D. $9$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

A set with $n$ elements has $2^n$ subsets. Here $n = 3$, so there are $2^3 = 8$:

$\varnothing$, $\{a\}$, $\{b\}$, $\{c\}$, $\{a, b\}$, $\{a, c\}$, $\{b, c\}$, $\{a, b, c\}$.

**Answer: C**

**Options**

## Q50 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Given that 20 students wrote a test out of 10 marks as shown in the following table:

| Mark               | 3   | 4   | 5   | 6   | 7   | 8   |
| ------------------ | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Number of students | 2   | 3   | 6   | 4   | 3   | 2   |

The probability of having a student who scored more than 6 marks is

**Options**

- A. $\frac{1}{4}$
- B. $\frac{3}{20}$
- C. $\frac{1}{5}$
- D. $\frac{6}{20}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

"More than 6" means 7 or 8 marks (6 itself is not included): $3 + 2 = 5$ students.

$$
P = \frac{5}{20} = \frac{1}{4}
$$

**Answer: A**

Including the students with 6 marks would give $\frac{9}{20}$, which is not "more than 6".
