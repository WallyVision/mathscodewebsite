## Q1 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The value of $\frac{1}{8} + \frac{2}{5}$ is

**Options**

- A. $\frac{3}{40}$
- B. $\frac{3}{13}$
- C. $\frac{21}{40}$
- D. $\frac{2}{40}$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Use the common denominator 40:

$$
\frac{1}{8} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{5}{40} + \frac{16}{40} = \frac{21}{40}
$$

**Answer: C**

Option B adds tops and bottoms separately, which is never how fractions add.

## Q2 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Given that $3^{-2} \times 3^p = 27$, then $p$ has the value

**Options**

- A. $-5$
- B. $5$
- C. $\frac{1}{5}$
- D. $-\frac{1}{5}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

$27 = 3^3$, and multiplying powers of 3 adds the indices:

$$
3^{p - 2} = 3^3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad p - 2 = 3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad p = 5
$$

**Answer: B**

**Options**

- A. $11101$
- B. $1011$
- C. $1101$
- D. $11011$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

$27 = 16 + 8 + 2 + 1$:

| 16  | 8   | 4   | 2   | 1   |

So $27 = 11011_2$.

**Answer: D**

Option A is $16 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 29$.

## Q11 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A trader sold a dress for 40,000 FCFA at a loss of 20%. The cost price of the dress, in FCFA, was

**Options**

- A. 50,000
- B. 8,000
- C. 32,000
- D. 60,000

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

A 20% loss means the selling price is 80% of the cost price:

$$
0.8 \times CP = 40\,000 \quad\Rightarrow\quad CP = \frac{40\,000}{0.8} = 50\,000 \text{ FCFA}
$$

Check: 20% of 50 000 is 10 000, and $50\,000 - 10\,000 = 40\,000$. ✓

**Answer: A**

Option C takes 20% off the **selling** price, but the loss is a percentage of the cost price.

## Q32 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

Given that $p = \frac{t - m}{t + m}$, then writing $m$ in terms of $p$ and $t$ gives

**Options**

- A. $\frac{t - pt}{p + 1}$
- B. $\frac{t - pt}{p - 1}$
- C. $\frac{t - pt}{2}$
- D. $\frac{t + pt}{p - 1}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Multiply out, collect the $m$ terms, then factorise:

$$
p(t + m) = t - m \quad\Rightarrow\quad pt + pm = t - m
$$

$$
pm + m = t - pt \quad\Rightarrow\quad m(p + 1) = t - pt \quad\Rightarrow\quad m = \frac{t - pt}{p + 1}
$$

**Answer: A**

## Q46 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Given the $2 \times 2$ matrix $P = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, the inverse of $P$ is

**Options**

- A. $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- B. $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -5 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- C. $\begin{pmatrix} -3 & 5 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
- D. $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 5 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

$\det P = 2 \times 3 - 1 \times 5 = 1$. Swap the leading diagonal, change the signs of the other two, and divide by the determinant (1):

$$
P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Check: $PP^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 - 5 & -2 + 2 \\ 15 - 15 & -5 + 6 \end{pmatrix} = I$. ✓

**Answer: A**

The median of the set of numbers $5, 3, 4, 10, 8, 4, 7, 12$ is

**Options**

- A. $6$
- B. $4$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

## Q50 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

In a table tennis competition, the probability of John winning is $\frac{2}{3}$ and that of Peter winning is $\frac{1}{2}$. The probability of John losing and Peter winning is

**Options**

- A. $\frac{5}{6}$
- B. $\frac{1}{6}$
- C. $\frac{1}{3}$
- D. $\frac{2}{3}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

$P(\text{John loses}) = 1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$. Taking the two results as independent, multiply:

$$
\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6}
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A adds instead of multiplying; "and" for independent events means multiply.
