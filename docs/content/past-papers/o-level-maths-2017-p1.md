## Q1 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Evaluating $\frac{5}{8} \div \frac{5}{4} - \frac{3}{4} \times 1\frac{1}{3}$ gives

**Options**

- A. $\frac{1}{2}$
- B. $-\frac{1}{2}$
- C. $\frac{25}{32}$
- D. $-\frac{7}{32}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Divide and multiply first, then subtract:

$$
\frac{5}{8} \div \frac{5}{4} = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{4}{3} = 1
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2} - 1 = -\frac{1}{2}
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A forgets that $1 > \frac{1}{2}$, so the result must be negative.

## Q20 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Given that each interior angle of a regular polygon is $160^\circ$, then the number of sides of the polygon is

**Options**

- A. $9$
- B. $10$
- C. $18$
- D. $20$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Each exterior angle is $180^\circ - 160^\circ = 20^\circ$. The exterior angles add up to $360^\circ$, so

$$
n = \frac{360^\circ}{20^\circ} = 18
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A divides $180^\circ$ by $20^\circ$; the exterior angles go once round, a full $360^\circ$.

## Q28 · difficulty 2

Given two position vectors $\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j}$ and $\overrightarrow{OQ} = 3\mathbf{i} - 9\mathbf{j}$, with $M$ the midpoint of $PQ$, then the position vector of $M$ is
