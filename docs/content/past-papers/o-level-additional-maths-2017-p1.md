## Q5 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The quadratic equation whose roots are $-2$ and $3$ is

- B. $x^2 + x - 6 = 0$

## Q7 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Which of the following is a factor of $x^3 + x^2 - 4x - 4$?

**Options**

- A. $x - 1$
- B. $x + 1$
- C. $x + 4$
- D. $x - 4$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Factor theorem: $(x + 1)$ is a factor if the expression is 0 at $x = -1$:

$$
-1 + 1 + 4 - 4 = 0 \ ✓
$$

In fact $x^3 + x^2 - 4x - 4 = x^2(x + 1) - 4(x + 1) = (x + 1)(x - 2)(x + 2)$.

**Answer: B**

At $x = 1$ the value is $-6$, so $(x - 1)$ is not a factor.

## Q10 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The sum, $S_n$, of an arithmetic progression is $n(2 - n)$. The 4th term is

**Options**

- A. $-11$
- B. $-8$
- C. $-5$
- D. $5$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

The 4th term is the sum of 4 terms minus the sum of 3:

$$
S_4 = 4(-2) = -8, \qquad S_3 = 3(-1) = -3
$$

$$
T_4 = S_4 - S_3 = -8 - (-3) = -5
$$

**Answer: C**

Option B is $S_4$ itself.

## Q13 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The first three terms, in ascending powers of $x$, in the binomial expansion of $(1 + x)^{-2}$ are

**Options**

- A. $1 + 2x + 3x^2 + \ldots$
- B. $1 - 2x + 3x^2 + \ldots$
- C. $1 + 2x - 3x^2 + \ldots$

$$

**Answer: B**

Option A is the expansion of $(1 - x)^{-2}$.

## Q37 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

An invariant line under the transformation $M$, where $M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, is

**Options**

- A. $3x + y = 0$
- B. $3x - y = 0$
- C. $x - 3y = 0$
- D. $x + 3y = 0$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

Try lines through the origin, $y = mx$. The point $(x, mx)$ maps to $((2 + 3m)x,\ (1 + 4m)x)$. For it to stay on the line:

$$
1 + 4m = m(2 + 3m) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3m^2 - 2m - 1 = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (3m + 1)(m - 1) = 0
$$

So $m = 1$ or $m = -\frac{1}{3}$. The line $y = -\frac{1}{3}x$ is $x + 3y = 0$.

Check: $(3, -1) \to (6 - 3, 3 - 4) = (3, -1)$, still on the line. ✓

**Answer: D**

## Q50 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

The area of the region bounded by the curve $y = x^2$ and the $x$-axis for $1 \le x \le 2$ is

**Options**

- A. $\frac{1}{3}$
- B. $\frac{7}{3}$
- C. $1$
- D. $3$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

$$
\int_1^2 x^2\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_1^2 = \frac{8}{3} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A is the area from 0 to 1 only.
