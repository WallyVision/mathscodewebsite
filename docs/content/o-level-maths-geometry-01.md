## Q1 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Two angles, $x$ and $3x$, lie next to each other on a straight line. Find $x$.

**Options**

- A. $90^\circ$
- B. $60^\circ$
- C. $45^\circ$
- D. $22.5^\circ$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Angles on a straight line add up to $180^\circ$.

$$
x + 3x = 180^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad 4x = 180^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 45^\circ
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A uses $360^\circ$, which is the sum of angles **around a point**, not on a straight line.

## Q2 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Two straight lines cross. One of the four angles formed is $65^\circ$. What is the angle vertically opposite it?

**Options**

- A. $65^\circ$
- B. $115^\circ$
- C. $25^\circ$
- D. $295^\circ$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Vertically opposite angles are **equal**, so the angle is $65^\circ$. **Answer: A**

Option B is the angle **next to** the $65^\circ$ angle (they lie on a straight line: $180^\circ - 65^\circ$). Option C treats the angles as complementary (adding to $90^\circ$).

## Q3 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Two co-interior (allied) angles between the parallel lines are $3x$ and $2x$. Find $x$.

**Options**

- A. $18^\circ$
- B. $72^\circ$
- C. $108^\circ$
- D. $36^\circ$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

Co-interior angles between parallel lines add up to $180^\circ$.

$$
3x + 2x = 180^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad 5x = 180^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 36^\circ
$$

**Answer: D**

Options B and C are the two angles themselves ($2x = 72^\circ$ and $3x = 108^\circ$), not $x$. Option A uses $90^\circ$ instead of $180^\circ$.

## Q4 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The angles of a triangle are $50^\circ$, $2x$ and $3x$. Find $x$.

**Options**

- A. $36^\circ$
- B. $26^\circ$
- C. $62^\circ$
- D. $52^\circ$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

The angles of a triangle add up to $180^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
50^\circ + 2x + 3x &= 180^\circ \\
5x &= 130^\circ \\
x &= 26^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A forgets the $50^\circ$ ($5x = 180^\circ$). Option D is $2x$, one of the angles, not $x$.

## Q5 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

In triangle $ABC$, angle $A = 48^\circ$ and angle $B = 67^\circ$. Side $BC$ is extended beyond $C$ to a point $D$. Find the exterior angle $ACD$.

**Options**

- A. $115^\circ$
- B. $65^\circ$
- C. $245^\circ$
- D. $132^\circ$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior **opposite** angles.

$$
\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B = 48^\circ + 67^\circ = 115^\circ
$$

(Check: the interior angle $C = 180^\circ - 115^\circ = 65^\circ$, and $65^\circ + 115^\circ = 180^\circ$ on the straight line $BCD$.) **Answer: A**

Option B is the **interior** angle at $C$, not the exterior angle.

## Q6 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

An isosceles triangle has an angle of $40^\circ$ between its two equal sides. Find each of the other two angles.

**Options**

- A. $40^\circ$
- B. $140^\circ$
- C. $70^\circ$
- D. $50^\circ$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

The two base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal, and the three angles add up to $180^\circ$.

$$
\text{each base angle} = \frac{180^\circ - 40^\circ}{2} = \frac{140^\circ}{2} = 70^\circ
$$

**Answer: C**

Option B forgets to share $140^\circ$ between the **two** base angles.

## Q7 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

What is the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon (6 sides)?

**Options**

- A. $1080^\circ$
- B. $540^\circ$
- C. $360^\circ$
- D. $720^\circ$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

The interior angles of an $n$-sided polygon add up to $(n - 2) \times 180^\circ$.

$$
(6 - 2) \times 180^\circ = 4 \times 180^\circ = 720^\circ
$$

(A hexagon can be split into 4 triangles from one vertex.) **Answer: D**

Option A uses $n \times 180^\circ$, forgetting to subtract 2. Option C is the sum of the **exterior** angles, which is $360^\circ$ for every polygon.

## Q8 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $30^\circ$. How many sides does the polygon have?

**Options**

- A. $6$
- B. $12$
- C. $10$
- D. $150$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

The exterior angles of any polygon add up to $360^\circ$, and in a regular polygon they are all equal.

$$
n = \frac{360^\circ}{30^\circ} = 12
$$

**Answer: B**

Option D is the size of each **interior** angle ($180^\circ - 30^\circ$), not the number of sides.

## Q9 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Find the size of each interior angle of a regular pentagon (5 sides).

**Options**

- A. $108^\circ$
- B. $72^\circ$
- C. $540^\circ$
- D. $120^\circ$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Each exterior angle is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{5} = 72^\circ$, so each interior angle is

$$
180^\circ - 72^\circ = 108^\circ
$$

(Or: the angle sum is $(5 - 2) \times 180^\circ = 540^\circ$, and $540^\circ \div 5 = 108^\circ$.) **Answer: A**

Option B is the exterior angle. Option C is the **sum** of all five interior angles. Option D is the interior angle of a regular **hexagon**.

## Q10 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

$O$ is the centre of a circle, and $A$, $B$ and $C$ are points on the circle. The angle $ACB$ at the circumference is $35^\circ$. Find the angle $AOB$ at the centre, subtended by the same arc $AB$.

**Options**

- A. $35^\circ$
- B. $17.5^\circ$
- C. $70^\circ$
- D. $145^\circ$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is **twice** the angle it subtends at the circumference.

$$
\angle AOB = 2 \times \angle ACB = 2 \times 35^\circ = 70^\circ
$$

**Answer: C**

Option B halves the angle instead of doubling it: the angle at the centre is always the larger one.

## Q11 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

$AB$ is a diameter of a circle and $C$ is another point on the circle. Angle $CAB = 28^\circ$. Find angle $ABC$.

**Options**

- A. $28^\circ$
- B. $62^\circ$
- C. $90^\circ$
- D. $152^\circ$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

The angle in a semicircle is a right angle, so $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$. The angles of triangle $ABC$ add up to $180^\circ$:

$$
\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 28^\circ = 62^\circ
$$

**Answer: B**

Option C is angle $ACB$, the angle in the semicircle, not the angle asked for. Option D forgets the right angle.

## Q12 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral (all four vertices lie on a circle). Angle $A = 105^\circ$. Find angle $C$, the angle opposite $A$.

**Options**

- A. $105^\circ$
- B. $255^\circ$
- C. $52.5^\circ$
- D. $75^\circ$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180^\circ$.

$$
\angle C = 180^\circ - 105^\circ = 75^\circ
$$

**Answer: D**

Option A assumes opposite angles are equal, which is true in a parallelogram but not in a cyclic quadrilateral. Option B subtracts from $360^\circ$.

## Q13 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

A tangent from a point $T$ touches a circle with centre $O$ at the point $P$. The radius of the circle is $5\,\text{cm}$ and $OT = 13\,\text{cm}$. Find the length of the tangent $TP$.

**Options**

- A. $8\,\text{cm}$
- B. $18\,\text{cm}$
- C. $12\,\text{cm}$
- D. $\sqrt{194}\,\text{cm}$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so $\angle OPT = 90^\circ$ and $OT$ is the hypotenuse of triangle $OPT$.

$$
TP^2 = OT^2 - OP^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144 \quad\Rightarrow\quad TP = 12\,\text{cm}
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A subtracts the lengths without squaring. Option D adds the squares, treating $TP$ as the hypotenuse.

## Q14 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Triangles $PQR$ and $XYZ$ are similar, with $PQ$ corresponding to $XY$ and $QR$ corresponding to $YZ$. $PQ = 4\,\text{cm}$, $XY = 6\,\text{cm}$ and $QR = 10\,\text{cm}$. Find $YZ$.

**Options**

- A. $15\,\text{cm}$
- B. $12\,\text{cm}$
- C. $6.7\,\text{cm}$
- D. $60\,\text{cm}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

In similar triangles, corresponding sides are in the same ratio. The scale factor from $PQR$ to $XYZ$ is

$$
\frac{XY}{PQ} = \frac{6}{4} = 1.5
$$

so $YZ = 10 \times 1.5 = 15\,\text{cm}$. **Answer: A**

Option B **adds** the difference ($6 - 4 = 2$) instead of multiplying by the scale factor. Option C uses the scale factor upside down.

## Q15 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

A trapezium has parallel sides of $8\,\text{cm}$ and $12\,\text{cm}$, and the perpendicular distance between them is $5\,\text{cm}$. Find its area.

**Options**

- A. $100\,\text{cm}^2$
- B. $50\,\text{cm}^2$
- C. $480\,\text{cm}^2$
- D. $40\,\text{cm}^2$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

$$
\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b)h = \tfrac{1}{2}(8 + 12) \times 5 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 20 \times 5 = 50\,\text{cm}^2
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A forgets the $\tfrac{1}{2}$. Option C multiplies all three lengths together.

## Q16 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Find the area of a circle of radius $7\,\text{cm}$. (Take $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.)

**Options**

- A. $44\,\text{cm}^2$
- B. $308\,\text{cm}^2$
- C. $22\,\text{cm}^2$
- D. $154\,\text{cm}^2$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

$$
A = \pi r^2 = \frac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 22 \times 7 = 154\,\text{cm}^2
$$

**Answer: D**

Option A is the **circumference** ($2\pi r = 44\,\text{cm}$), a length, not an area. Option B uses the diameter instead of the radius in $\pi r \times d$.

## Q17 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

Find the length of the arc of a circle of radius $14\,\text{cm}$ that subtends an angle of $90^\circ$ at the centre. (Take $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.)

**Options**

- A. $154\,\text{cm}$
- B. $88\,\text{cm}$
- C. $22\,\text{cm}$
- D. $44\,\text{cm}$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

An arc is a fraction of the whole circumference: the angle at the centre over $360^\circ$.

$$
\text{arc length} = \frac{90}{360} \times 2\pi r = \frac{1}{4} \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14 = \frac{1}{4} \times 88 = 22\,\text{cm}
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A is the **area** of the sector ($\tfrac{1}{4} \pi r^2$), not a length. Option B is the whole circumference. Option D is half the circumference.

## Q18 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Find the area of a sector of a circle of radius $6\,\text{cm}$ with an angle of $60^\circ$ at the centre. Leave your answer in terms of $\pi$.

**Options**

- A. $6\pi\,\text{cm}^2$
- B. $2\pi\,\text{cm}^2$
- C. $36\pi\,\text{cm}^2$
- D. $12\pi\,\text{cm}^2$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

$$
\text{sector area} = \frac{60}{360} \times \pi r^2 = \frac{1}{6} \times \pi \times 36 = 6\pi\,\text{cm}^2
$$

**Answer: A**

Option B is the **arc length** ($\tfrac{1}{6} \times 2\pi r$). Option C is the area of the whole circle. Option D is the full circumference.

## Q19 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A cylinder has radius $3\,\text{cm}$ and height $10\,\text{cm}$. Find its volume, in terms of $\pi$.

**Options**

- A. $30\pi\,\text{cm}^3$
- B. $60\pi\,\text{cm}^3$
- C. $300\pi\,\text{cm}^3$
- D. $90\pi\,\text{cm}^3$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

$$
V = \pi r^2 h = \pi \times 3^2 \times 10 = 90\pi\,\text{cm}^3
$$

**Answer: D**

Option A forgets to square the radius. Option B is the **curved surface area** ($2\pi r h$), in $\text{cm}^2$. Option C squares the height instead of the radius.

## Q20 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

Two similar shapes have corresponding lengths in the ratio $1 : 3$. The smaller shape has an area of $5\,\text{cm}^2$. Find the area of the larger shape.

**Options**

- A. $15\,\text{cm}^2$
- B. $45\,\text{cm}^2$
- C. $135\,\text{cm}^2$
- D. $1.7\,\text{cm}^2$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

If lengths are multiplied by $k$, areas are multiplied by $k^2$ (and volumes by $k^3$). Here $k = 3$, so areas are multiplied by $3^2 = 9$.

$$
5 \times 9 = 45\,\text{cm}^2
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A uses the length ratio for the area. Option C cubes the scale factor, which is the rule for **volumes**. Option D divides instead of multiplying.
