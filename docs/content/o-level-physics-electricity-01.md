## Q1 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

A steady current of $2\,\text{A}$ flows in a circuit for $30\,\text{s}$. How much charge passes a point in the circuit?

**Options**

- A. $15\,\text{C}$
- B. $60\,\text{C}$
- C. $0.067\,\text{C}$
- D. $32\,\text{C}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Charge = current × time.

$$
Q = It = 2\,\text{A} \times 30\,\text{s} = 60\,\text{C}
$$

**Answer: B**

Option A divides instead of multiplying. Option D adds the numbers.

## Q2 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

One volt is the same as

**Options**

- A. one joule per coulomb.
- B. one coulomb per second.
- C. one joule per second.
- D. one ohm per ampere.

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Potential difference is the energy transferred per unit charge: $V = \dfrac{W}{Q}$. So $1\,\text{V} = 1\,\text{J C}^{-1}$. **Answer: A**

Option B is the definition of the **ampere** (current). Option C is the **watt** (power).

## Q3 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

A p.d. of $12\,\text{V}$ across a resistor drives a current of $3\,\text{A}$ through it. What is its resistance?

**Options**

- A. $36\,\Omega$
- B. $0.25\,\Omega$
- C. $4\,\Omega$
- D. $15\,\Omega$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

$$
R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{12\,\text{V}}{3\,\text{A}} = 4\,\Omega
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A multiplies $V$ by $I$. Option B divides $I$ by $V$.

## Q4 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Resistors of $4\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$ are connected **in series** to a $20\,\text{V}$ supply of negligible internal resistance. What current flows?

**Options**

- A. $0.5\,\text{A}$
- B. $5\,\text{A}$
- C. $8.3\,\text{A}$
- D. $2\,\text{A}$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

In series, resistances add: $R = 4 + 6 = 10\,\Omega$.

$$
I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{20}{10} = 2\,\text{A}
$$

**Answer: D**

Option B uses only the $4\,\Omega$ resistor. Option C uses the **parallel** combination ($2.4\,\Omega$) by mistake.

## Q5 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

What is the combined resistance of a $6\,\Omega$ resistor and a $3\,\Omega$ resistor connected **in parallel**?

**Options**

- A. $9\,\Omega$
- B. $2\,\Omega$
- C. $0.5\,\Omega$
- D. $4.5\,\Omega$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

For resistors in parallel:

$$
\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

So $R = 2\,\Omega$. **Answer: B**

Option C stops at $\dfrac{1}{R} = 0.5$ and forgets to turn it upside down. Option A adds them as if in series.

A useful check: the combined resistance in parallel is always **smaller than the smallest** resistor.

## Q6 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

How should an ammeter and a voltmeter be connected to measure the current through a lamp and the p.d. across it?

**Options**

- A. Ammeter in series with the lamp; voltmeter in parallel with the lamp
- B. Both in series with the lamp
- C. Ammeter in parallel with the lamp; voltmeter in series with the lamp
- D. Both in parallel with the lamp

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

An **ammeter** measures the current flowing **through** the lamp, so the same current must pass through it: it goes **in series**.

A **voltmeter** compares the energy per charge on **either side** of the lamp: it goes **in parallel** (across the lamp). **Answer: A**

## Q7 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

A lamp connected to a $240\,\text{V}$ supply carries a current of $0.25\,\text{A}$. What is its power?

**Options**

- A. $960\,\text{W}$
- B. $240.25\,\text{W}$
- C. $0.001\,\text{W}$
- D. $60\,\text{W}$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

$$
P = IV = 0.25 \times 240 = 60\,\text{W}
$$

**Answer: D**

Option A divides $V$ by $I$ (that gives the resistance in ohms, $960\,\Omega$, not the power).

## Q8 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

A $2\,\text{kW}$ water heater is used for $3$ hours every day for $30$ days. Electricity costs $100\,\text{FCFA}$ per kWh. What is the cost of using the heater?

**Options**

- A. $600\,\text{FCFA}$
- B. $6000\,\text{FCFA}$
- C. $18\,000\,\text{FCFA}$
- D. $18\,000\,000\,\text{FCFA}$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

Energy in kilowatt-hours = power in kW × time in hours.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= 2\,\text{kW} \times (3 \times 30)\,\text{h} = 2 \times 90 = 180\,\text{kWh} \\
\text{cost} &= 180 \times 100 = 18\,000\,\text{FCFA}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Answer: C**

Option A is the cost for **one day** only. Option B forgets the 3 hours per day. Option D uses $2000\,\text{W}$ instead of $2\,\text{kW}$: the kWh unit needs the power in **kilowatts**.

## Q9 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

An electric kettle is rated $2.3\,\text{kW}$, $230\,\text{V}$. Fuses of $3\,\text{A}$, $5\,\text{A}$, $13\,\text{A}$ and $30\,\text{A}$ are available. Which fuse is most suitable?

**Options**

- A. $3\,\text{A}$
- B. $5\,\text{A}$
- C. $13\,\text{A}$
- D. $30\,\text{A}$

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

First find the normal working current.

$$
I = \frac{P}{V} = \frac{2300\,\text{W}}{230\,\text{V}} = 10\,\text{A}
$$

Choose the fuse rated **just above** the normal current: $13\,\text{A}$. **Answer: C**

Options A and B would melt every time the kettle is switched on. Option D would let a dangerously large current flow before melting, so it would not protect the kettle or its cable.

## Q10 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

What is the purpose of the **earth wire** in an appliance with a metal case?

**Options**

- A. To carry the current into the appliance
- B. To carry current safely to the ground if the metal case becomes live
- C. To make the appliance use less energy
- D. To keep the voltage of the supply steady

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

If a fault makes the metal case live, the earth wire gives the current a low-resistance path to the ground. The large current that flows melts the fuse (or trips the circuit breaker) and cuts off the supply, so nobody touching the case gets a shock. **Answer: B**

Option A describes the **live** wire.

## Q11 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A polythene rod becomes **negatively** charged when it is rubbed with a cloth. Why?

**Options**

- A. Electrons move from the cloth to the rod.
- B. Protons move from the rod to the cloth.
- C. Electrons move from the rod to the cloth.
- D. The rubbing creates new negative charges.

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Only **electrons** (negative) move when objects are charged by friction; protons stay fixed in the nuclei. The rod gains electrons from the cloth, so the rod becomes negative and the cloth is left equally positive. **Answer: A**

Option B is wrong because protons do not move. Option C would make the rod positive. Option D is wrong because charge is never created, only transferred.

## Q12 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

Two balloons are both charged **negatively** and are held close to each other. What happens?

**Options**

- A. They attract each other.
- B. There is no force between them.
- C. They attract, then repel.
- D. They repel each other.

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. Both balloons are negative (like charges), so they **repel**. **Answer: D**

## Q13 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A current of $2\,\text{A}$ flows through a $5\,\Omega$ resistor. At what rate is energy converted to heat in the resistor?

**Options**

- A. $10\,\text{W}$
- B. $20\,\text{W}$
- C. $50\,\text{W}$
- D. $2.5\,\text{W}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

$$
P = I^2 R = 2^2 \times 5 = 4 \times 5 = 20\,\text{W}
$$

(Or: $V = IR = 10\,\text{V}$, then $P = IV = 2 \times 10 = 20\,\text{W}$.) **Answer: B**

Option A is $IR$, the p.d. in volts, not the power. Option C squares the resistance instead of the current.

## Q14 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A wire of a certain material and thickness has a resistance $R$. What is the resistance of a wire of the same material and thickness but **twice as long**?

**Options**

- A. $2R$
- B. $\dfrac{R}{2}$
- C. $R$
- D. $4R$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Resistance is proportional to length: a longer wire gives electrons further to travel through the material, so doubling the length doubles the resistance. **Answer: A**

Option B is what happens when the **cross-sectional area** is doubled instead.

## Q15 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

The light falling on a light-dependent resistor (LDR) becomes brighter. What happens to its resistance?

**Options**

- A. It increases.
- B. It stays the same.
- C. It falls to zero.
- D. It decreases.

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

An LDR has a **high** resistance in the dark and a **lower** resistance in bright light. More light means lower resistance. **Answer: D**

Option C is wrong: the resistance gets lower but never reaches zero.

## Q16 · difficulty 1

**Stem**

What is the main property of a diode in a circuit?

**Options**

- A. It lets current flow equally in both directions.
- B. It lets current flow only when the supply is alternating.
- C. It lets current flow in one direction only.
- D. It lets current flow only if a resistor is connected in parallel with it.

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

A diode has a very low resistance in one direction (forward bias) and a very high resistance in the other (reverse bias), so current flows **in one direction only**. This is why diodes are used to change alternating current into direct current (rectification). **Answer: C**

## Q17 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

As the current through a filament lamp increases, how does its resistance change, and why?

**Options**

- A. It stays constant, because the lamp obeys Ohm's law.
- B. It decreases, because more charge flows.
- C. It increases, because the filament gets hotter.
- D. It increases, because the p.d. across it decreases.

**Correct:** C

**Solution**

A larger current heats the filament. In a hotter metal the atoms vibrate more and get in the way of the moving electrons more often, so the resistance **increases**. That is why the current–voltage graph of a filament lamp is a curve that bends towards the voltage axis. **Answer: C**

Option A is true only for an ohmic conductor kept at **constant temperature**, which a lamp filament is not.

## Q18 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

Three identical lamps are connected **in parallel** across a $12\,\text{V}$ battery. What is the p.d. across each lamp?

**Options**

- A. $4\,\text{V}$
- B. $12\,\text{V}$
- C. $36\,\text{V}$
- D. $0\,\text{V}$

**Correct:** B

**Solution**

Components connected in parallel all have the **same p.d.** across them, equal to the supply p.d. So each lamp has $12\,\text{V}$ across it. **Answer: B**

Option A shares the voltage between the lamps, which happens only when they are connected **in series**.

## Q19 · difficulty 3

**Stem**

A $2\,\text{k}\Omega$ resistor and a $4\,\text{k}\Omega$ resistor are connected in series across a $12\,\text{V}$ supply. What is the p.d. across the $4\,\text{k}\Omega$ resistor?

**Options**

- A. $4\,\text{V}$
- B. $6\,\text{V}$
- C. $24\,\text{V}$
- D. $8\,\text{V}$

**Correct:** D

**Solution**

In series, the supply p.d. is shared in proportion to the resistances (a potential divider).

$$
V_{4\,\text{k}\Omega} = \frac{4}{2 + 4} \times 12 = \frac{4}{6} \times 12 = 8\,\text{V}
$$

Check: the $2\,\text{k}\Omega$ resistor gets $4\,\text{V}$, and $8 + 4 = 12\,\text{V}$. **Answer: D**

Option A is the p.d. across the **other** resistor. Option B shares the voltage equally, ignoring the resistances.

## Q20 · difficulty 2

**Stem**

A $9\,\text{V}$ battery drives a charge of $5\,\text{C}$ around a circuit. How much energy does the battery transfer?

**Options**

- A. $45\,\text{J}$
- B. $1.8\,\text{J}$
- C. $0.56\,\text{J}$
- D. $14\,\text{J}$

**Correct:** A

**Solution**

Energy = charge × p.d. (e.m.f.).

$$
W = QV = 5 \times 9 = 45\,\text{J}
$$

**Answer: A**

Option B divides $V$ by $Q$. Option D adds the numbers.
